                  How these numbers are worked out
                  • Everything comes from the game's own log files, copied from the Quest through Eleven's File Sharing. The Quest keeps only its most recent launches, so a copy of every log is kept on your computer. Nothing is sent anywhere: the numbers are worked out there, by code anyone can read.
                  • A session is a run of AI matches with less than 45 minutes between one ending and the next starting.
                  • The session report sets each session against all your earlier points at the same AI level. A change is called out when a session of that size would show a gap that large by chance only about one time in ten, so about one line in ten is still luck. The notes are written by the same code that builds the data.
                  • The training plan is built for the AI level you played most in your last five sessions. Now is your latest sessions at that level, taken together until they hold 1,000 points. The target is the same number in the matches you won at that level. What an item is worth is the number of tries you get in a match, times the gap between now and the target, times what one more success has been worth in points over all your points at that level. That assumes the extra balls you land win as often as the ones you land today, and that the AI plays the same way, so read it as a ranking and not as a promise. An arrow marks a number that moved further from your earlier sessions than chance explains about nine times in ten. Speeds are the ball's speed just after the hit. The plan keeps no history, so to say when an item joined it, the same sums are done again as they would have stood after each earlier session at that level. Then is the item's number over the recent sessions as they stood when it first made the list; since is every session at that level played after that one. An item joins when its number is low, and a low number is partly bad luck, so a small rise afterwards would come even without practice.
                  • In Serves and returns, depth is where the serve first bounces on the receiver's half: short is within 50 cm of the net, half-long is 50 cm to 1 m from it, long is the last 37 cm. A serve has topspin or backspin when it carries more than 20 r/s of it, otherwise little spin. A point is won by the return when the AI does not reach it or misses its next shot; it is lost to the AI's next shot when you do not reach that shot or miss it. The same goes for your serve and third ball. Rows and serve types with fewer than 30 serves are left out, and the written notes use only groups of 50 or more.
                  • The point replay moves the ball in straight lines between the contacts the game logged, at the logged times, so a ball that curved with sidespin is drawn straight. The ball is drawn larger the higher it is; its height between contacts is worked out from the contact heights and gravity. When the log stops with the ball in the air, the last stretch carries on the way the ball was going for 0.4 seconds. The 3D view curves each flight instead: the log gives the ball's velocity just after every hit and as it arrives at every contact, and the curve is the smooth path that leaves and arrives with those. Where the two can be compared, on a shot from bat to table, this path and a simpler one sit about 2 cm apart at mid-flight. The log has nothing between contacts, no bats and no players, and the spin shown is the spin as the ball left the bat.
                  • The landing call measures from where the log says the ball met the table to the nearest edge of that half. A ball that missed the table is measured where its drawn flight comes down to the height of the table, and the height over the net is read off the drawn flight where it crosses the net, so those two are estimates, good to a few centimetres.
                  • The shot explorer lists every shot you hit, and every ball that landed on your half and you never touched. The ball that came at you is described by the shot before yours: its spin as it left the bat (topspin or backspin above 20 r/s, otherwise little spin), fast when it left the bat at 10 m/s or more, its side by where it bounced on your half (the middle is the 50 cm down the centre of the table; a ball that did not bounce is placed where you met it), and its depth as for serves. A miss is into the net when the ball never got over it, long when the line of the shot passes the end of the table between its sides, and wide otherwise. Where your misses pile up is worked out for the level of the training plan.
                  • Rolling results take your matches at one AI level in the order you played them. The band is the range a share sits in 9 times in 10 (the Wilson interval), counting each match, or each point, as a separate try; points inside one match are not quite separate tries, so the band for points is a little too narrow. From the first match of a session to the last uses sessions with three or more matches at one level, and sets each match against the others of that session and level; a place in the session is called clearly better or worse when a gap that large would turn up by chance about one time in ten.
                  • Videos are the headset recording of a session, uploaded whole to YouTube as unlisted: only someone with the link can open one. A recording is lined up with the log by sound, sliding the log's contact times along the soundtrack until they sit on the clicks of the ball. A point's link starts two seconds before its toss. Sessions that were not recorded have no links.
                  • The highlight reel is cut from the same recording by code, from the log alone. Each point scores two for every shot in the rally, one for every m/s over 10 on your winning shot, and extra for a point won at game point, late in a close game, or to win a match. Points you won need three shots or more; up to two rallies of six shots or more that you lost go in too. The best-scoring points are taken until the reel is about 90 seconds long, then shown in the order they were played, one rally fading into the next. The misses reel is cut the same way. Your shots are grouped by the ball that came at you, as in the shot explorer, and the kinds of ball you missed or did not reach most often in that session come first. For each kind it shows up to four of the misses, spread over the session, and then up to two occasions when your shot against the same ball landed.
                  • The metrics use 11hero.com's definitions. They were checked against 11hero on a real online match, with both players read from the game's log: every hit came out the same (speed, spin, shot type, whether it landed and whether it won the point), and so did 37 of the 42 numbers on its page. The other five are the shares of points won by each kind of shot, which move a little because 11hero counts one more, empty point at the end of the match.
                  • Speed is the ball's speed just after the hit, in metres per second. Spin is in revolutions per second; topspin and backspin are the part of the spin that rolls the ball forward or back. Topspin and backspin averages count only hits with more than 20 r/s of it.
                  • A shot landed when its first bounce was on the other side. A serve landed when it bounced on your side and then the other.
                  • Shot types follow 11hero's rule: backspin over the table is a push, backspin away from it a chop; 50 r/s or more of spin is a loop (a fast loop at 10.5 m/s or more); anything else is a drive. An open-up is a drive or loop against a ball that had backspin.
                  • Left and right are as you face the AI. You hold the paddle in your right hand, so your left is your backhand side. You hold the paddle in your left hand, so your right is your backhand side. The game's paddle settings in the logs say which hand holds the paddle; elevendash config hand=left (or right) sets it instead. The direction was checked against a video recording.
                  • The opening line, the second scoreboard and What decides your points cover your main level: the AI level with the most points in your last five sessions. The findings there are written by code with fixed rules. A headline calls one thing better than another only when the gap is at least 5 percentage points (3 for landing rates, which rest on thousands of shots, and 0.5 m/s for speed). A finding with fewer than 3 matches won and 3 lost, 30 serves or 50 shots of a type behind it is left out.
                  • The AI level is the game's difficulty setting; 100 is legendary. On days you changed level, the progress chart labels the day with the higher one.
                  • Lets are left out. Online matches appear in the match explorer but not in the AI totals.
                  • Small groups swing a lot. The zone map and the rally-length chart show how many serves or points each number covers, and the zone map colours only zones with 30 serves or more.
